Hindu Marriage Registration Guide

A careful overview of Hindu marriage registration, state-rule checks, evidence categories, witnesses and finding the correct registrar.

How ceremony and registration relate

The Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 addresses ceremonies and registration in separate provisions. India Code's section 8 shows that registration rules are made at state level. A religious ceremony and an administrative registration record are therefore connected but not interchangeable planning tasks.

What to verify locally

Ask which law and state rules apply, which office has jurisdiction, whether an online start is available, who must attend and which originals must be shown. Do not rely on a checklist from another state.

Evidence categories to ask about

An authority may request categories such as identity, age, residence, ceremony evidence, photographs, declarations or prior-marital-status records. These are question prompts only; obtain the current exact list from the official portal.

Witness planning questions

Confirm the required number, eligibility, attendance, identity evidence and whether witnesses must have attended the ceremony. Do not assume a relative or friend automatically qualifies.

Common application mistakes

Using the wrong jurisdiction, inconsistent spellings, relying on expired screenshots, arriving without required originals, or paying through an unofficial link can delay an application.

Protect identity records

Do not upload identification numbers or document scans to ShaadiGuide. Use a verified government portal and check the web address before entering personal information or paying.

Helpful answers

Frequently asked questions

Is this legal advice?

No. It is an organisational guide. Use the applicable government portal and obtain qualified legal advice for individual questions.

Are documents and witness rules the same across India?

No. The responsible authority and requirements can vary by law, state, district and circumstances.

Should I pay an agent?

Use the official portal or office first. Verify any fee and approved payment method with the authority, keep receipts and never share identity documents through an unverified service.

When was this page verified?

The linked primary government sources were checked on 20 July 2026. Recheck them because procedures can change.
